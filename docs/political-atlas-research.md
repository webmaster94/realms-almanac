# Political atlas sources and label corrections

Checked 4 October 2026. This is a research audit of the 1357-entry `qa/continuous-atlas/catalog.json`, pinned Toril GIS `2026-09-24_1`, and directly inspected source maps. No runtime catalog, production tiles, or application code was changed.

## Use registered source polygons, with a country classification

The best available boundary geometry is already in `qa/gis/srf_con_named_regions_pg.geojson`. Its 61 polygons combine countries, provinces, forests, island groups, and broad geographic regions. Faerûn entries identify their source as `WotC Faerûn Political 3.5e`; Kara-Tur entries usually identify FRIA. Neither `Region` nor `Macroregion` means country. [Pinned named-region dataset](https://downloads.geospatial-grimoire.com/toril-gis/packages/2026-09-24_1/geojson/srf_con_named_regions_pg.geojson)

The dataset creator explains that the underlying 3e cartography was aligned to the FRIA framework with more than 900 control points and a thin-plate-spline transform. The same transformation supports the 3e political map. This makes the existing vectors preferable to tracing a stylized political reference or stretching a new image over the current terrain. The creator also explicitly excludes 4e geography from this framework. That is a statement about spatial alignment, not proof of contemporary borders. [Creator's edition-compatibility documentation](https://www.geospatial-grimoire.com/worlds/toril/gis/docs/edition-compatibility/)

For comparison, I downloaded the [registered WotC political TIFF](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/geospatial-grimoire/forgotten-realms-rasters/15fc3e452a8c6ad4061c8f61a7aadae862923938/3-modern-wotc/WotC%20-%20Faerun%20Political_3E.tif) into `qa/political-atlas/faerun-political-3e-georeferenced.tif`. It is 4898 × 4032, SHA-256 `080f919b523e0b36aa1220165b9cfa3e92498cc734f297df0752a06fe3998c32`. Its embedded tags specify Toril GCS, FRIA, pixel-is-area, a pixel scale of `0.013030005483341427` degrees, and upper-left outer corner `[-86.74348557818848, 55.8387536311555]`. The preview shows irregular warping and black gaps. It labels forests and geographic regions within the same political graphic. Use it to review provenance and missing geometry, not as a clean replacement basemap or evidence that every enclosed area is a sovereign state.

`qa/political-atlas/country-geometry-candidates.geojson` preserves 21 original source geometries without simplifying, moving, or inventing boundaries. An explicit editorial list selects territorial states described in the source GIS: Aglarond, Amn, Calimshan, Cormyr, Damara, Dambrath, Halruaa, Impiltur, Lantan, Lapaliiya, Luiren, Mulhorand, Rashemen, Sembia, Tabot, Tashalar, Tethyr, Thay, Thesk, T'u Lung, and Unther. This is a candidate list, not a completeness claim. The file marks every candidate `currentEraBoundaryVerified: false` and `autoPublish: false`. Source descriptions establish how the GIS interprets the entity; they do not independently establish 1502 DR borders. In particular, returned or war-torn realms need an era decision before their older borders are represented as current.

Do not give automatic national borders to Sword Coast, Western Heartlands, Lake of Steam, Golden Water, Moonsea, Vilhon Reach, Dragon Coast, Dalelands, The Vast, Chult, Nelanther Isles, Moonshae Isles, Chondalwood, Wealdath, Smoking Mountains, the Shaar, or provinces merely because they have polygons. The audit also leaves fragmented or era-sensitive cases such as Chessenta, Narfell, Vaasa, and Shadovar outside its conservative default candidate list. This is a review decision, not a claim that these names have never denoted political entities. `region-classification-review.json` lists the decision for all 61 source records.

## Analogous current catalog errors

`qa/political-atlas/catalog-audit.json` records 11 concrete findings plus duplicate and missing-label recommendations.

- Nine rivers are classified as `Water`: Chionthar, Delimbiyr River, Dessarin River, River Mirar, River Rauvin, River Surbrin, The Laughingflow, Unicorn Run, and Winding Water. Their reviewed Sword Coast text placements should become river labels. Being beside a line rather than inside a lake is normal for printed river text.
- Chionthar and Winding Water have both `Water` and `River` entries. Mirar and River Mirar refer to the same river under different source naming. Deepwash and The Deepwash are duplicate water names. Use explicit aliases and typed feature IDs, not unrestricted name-based collapsing across cities, regions, and water bodies.
- Western Ocean currently sits at `[163, -13]`. Its named source polygon lies west of Maztica, with negative longitudes between -180 and approximately -117.215. The source interior anchor is `[-154.36132718983973, -15.012343808236402]`. Correct this legacy ocean placement. All other exact-name current `Water` anchors tested against the corresponding source lake/ocean polygons fell inside them.
- Golden Water repeats the Lake of Steam pattern: only a broad macroregion exists in the current catalog. I inspected the printed blue water label in the registered Faerûn source. Its approximate original text center `[9307, 5614]` transforms to `[-25.71665746024361, 16.6216573324904]`. The audit JSON includes this water-label candidate and its review crop. This text placement is not a bay boundary.
- Moonsea and Vilhon Reach legitimately have broader regional usage as well as water usage. Preserve that distinction in search metadata; only their water features should receive water styling, and neither broad regional polygon should automatically receive a national border.

The nine river names and Golden Water were read in the inspected source cartography, including the [official Sword Coast source](https://media.wizards.com/2015/images/dnd/resources/20151117_Sword-Coast-Map.jpg) and [registered Faerûn source](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/geospatial-grimoire/forgotten-realms-rasters/15fc3e452a8c6ad4061c8f61a7aadae862923938/4-community/AIF%20%282020%29%20-%20Faerun_v2.jpg). A generic test for water labels touching land would wrongly flag river text and ice-covered waters. Test water bodies against the appropriate named polygon; test rivers against their line source when one exists.

## Implementation recommendations

Keep three independent fields: entity type, geometry role, and border evidence. For example, Elturgard can have `kind: Country`, a printed-text label anchor, and no available border polygon. Lake of Steam can have both a sea geometry and a broader historical region, each with its own ID. Coordinates must stay in the existing FRIA system.

Use the reviewed country allowlist to select original polygon rings. Never use label bounds, feature bounding boxes, convex hulls, or the outer edge of an arbitrary regional mask as a country's boundary. Render a narrow colored edge clipped to the country's polygon, with opacity falling inward over a short screen-space distance. Keep the central terrain readable and remove the border treatment outside the chosen regional zoom range. Screen-space fade width is a styling choice; it must not move the geographic border. Clip color away from unrelated water where the source polygon includes coastal sea. Do not smooth across missing or disputed geometry to close a border that the source does not establish.

For the immediate fix, the five already classified countries have source polygons. Elturgard's label can be corrected without a border. The additional candidate states can be reviewed against the campaign's chosen era. The supplied stylized political reference can guide colors and hierarchy, but its outline shapes should not silently become canonical geography.

Rebuild these research files with `python -X utf8 qa/political-atlas/build-research-candidates.py`. The script touches only `qa/political-atlas`; all JSON and text reads/writes use UTF-8 explicitly.

## Elturgard trace and measured registration

The requested follow-up registration is now in `qa/political-atlas/elturgard-boundary-candidate.geojson`. It contains one valid polygon with 324 vertices. `register-elturgard.py` extracts the visible purple outer enclosure from the creator's source, closes only small pixel gaps, and simplifies the contour by 1.5 pixels at quarter source resolution. The yellow trace in `elturgard-source-trace-qa.jpg` follows the drawn enclosure, including its forest indentations. It does not connect settlement points or use a bounding box.

Seven individually measured source landmarks were matched to current native Sword Coast marker coordinates: Elturel, Scornubel, Berdusk, Baldur's Gate, Triel, Fort Morninglord, and the Boareskyr Bridge crossing. Source marker crops and the native comparison remain in the QA folder. A least-squares local affine maps the trace into native Sword Coast pixels, then its established georeferencing converts those points into FRIA longitude/latitude. No existing city coordinates were changed.

The maps do not agree exactly. The affine training RMSE is **46.9 native Sword Coast pixels**, with a maximum of **103.2 pixels at Triel**. Leave-one-out RMSE is **96.0 pixels**. A homography lowers training error to 41.4 pixels but raises leave-one-out error to 556.1 pixels, so it was rejected. The 2025 source places Triel substantially farther north of Elturel than the 2015 source does. These residuals represent source cartographic differences as well as measurement uncertainty; the resulting boundary is illustrative.

All five tested interior settlements/sites, Elturel, Scornubel, Berdusk, Triel, and Fort Morninglord, remain inside the registered polygon. Baldur's Gate is outside by 281.6 native pixels and the current Iriaebor label position by 273.1 pixels. Both city markers are also visibly outside the original creator outline. The 2015 source has no visible Iriaebor dot at the reviewed label, so the target check uses the catalog's explicitly identified printed-label center. The newer source does have a visible Iriaebor circle outside the border.

Boareskyr Bridge exposes the remaining northern alignment error: its target marker lies 20.7 native pixels outside the registered line, while the creator's bridge lies just inside its original enclosure. Do not silently move that marker or stretch the traced border to conceal the discrepancy. At approximately 0.087 coordinate degrees this is small relative to the country's extent, but it matters for claims about exact border crossings. The candidate records its approximate status. `elturgard-registration.json` contains all measured controls, both model comparisons, leave-one-out residuals, original pixel contour, and inclusion checks. `elturgard-registered-native-qa.jpg` and `elturgard-registration-contact.jpg` provide the visual check.

The limited review of the other 21 country candidates is in `country-1501-coverage-review.json`. Fourteen are visibly named national areas in the 1501 source; Lapaliiya is only partly in its southern coverage, and six lie outside or are not legibly available in that coverage. The strongest concrete mismatch is Tethyr: the existing Tethyr polygon has zero overlap with the separately classified Wealdath polygon, while the new map places Wealdath inside Tethyr's larger national envelope. Thus the old political/cultural partition is not interchangeable with a modern complete country boundary. The new source also draws Elturgard, Najara, Evereska, Vesperin, Turmish, Erlkazar, Chondath, Sespech, and Vaasa, which the conservative 21-country candidate set lacks. Thay's internal colored tharch subdivisions must not become separate country borders. A global geometric comparison was not attempted; these are visible source differences and coverage gaps, not measured area-change claims.

## Final water-caption and painted-map check

`verified-water-label-patch.json` proposes four water records and four same-name regional caption suppressions, without touching the runtime catalog. Vilhon Reach is unequivocally a water label in the inspected source artwork. The registered official Sword Coast text center is pixel `[9637,5840]`, longitude `-52.35386231700322`, latitude `29.52225881302474`. The original 1357-entry catalog already has this as `thevilhonreach-water`, named `The Vilhon Reach`; preserve it through canonical-name normalization instead of collapsing it into the broader `Region`. The independent registered AIF text center is `[-50.778374337883285,29.95430870508816]`. These are two valid text placements within the same long water body, not two different seas. [Official source artwork](https://media.wizards.com/2015/images/dnd/resources/20151117_Sword-Coast-Map.jpg), [registered AIF source](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/geospatial-grimoire/forgotten-realms-rasters/15fc3e452a8c6ad4061c8f61a7aadae862923938/4-community/AIF%20%282020%29%20-%20Faerun_v2.jpg)

The proposed visible names are Vilhon Reach, Lake of Steam, Golden Water, and Moonsea, all `Water`. Their broader same-name regions should retain search metadata with `searchOnly: true`. The other apparent regional water-name candidate, The Frozen Sea, prints over bare northern terrain beside the ice edge in the official source; it should not become an open-water caption based on the word Sea alone. The remaining name matches, such as The Reaching Woods and Waterdeep, are not water-body records.

Using the exact mosaic bounds, I sampled 110 current or proposed water-anchor rows within `faerun-final.png`. Both Vilhon placements, the new Lake of Steam geometry anchor, the Golden Water source anchor, and Moonsea all have blue-water centers and a 100% water-color score in their surrounding 15 × 15 pixel patches. Contact-sheet inspection confirms the points sit within the painted water. The raw result is `painted-water-anchor-audit.json`; three `painted-water-review-*.jpg` sheets show the flagged locations and key verified labels.

Thirty-seven rows trigger the deliberately simple color check. Several are rivers with normal bank-side text positions or icy waters, so this is not a count of 37 bad coordinates. The material visual flags are Alamber Sea, whose anchor lies on a painted forested delta south of the Thay overlay; Wyvernwater, whose anchor lies just inland of the painted lake; and Icewind lake anchors whose painted surroundings differ from the source lake geometry. The Endless Ice Sea's AIF top-edge text anchor also appears at an inland river junction in the painting and needs a geographic review before use as a sea location. Other small lakes and glacial rifts need local source comparison. No point was moved to satisfy the color detector, and no artwork was modified.
