# Toril crystal sphere and phlogiston

Checked 3 October 2026. This supplements [atlas research](atlas-research.md) and [Realmspace data](realmspace.md). The requested title is `Toril Crystal Sphere`; `Realmspace` remains a useful subtitle. This atlas uses the classic AD&D cosmology requested by the campaign.

## Verified classic description

The SJR2 *Realmspace* preview establishes the following:

- The shell has a 3.2-billion-mile radius, twice H'Catha's orbital radius. Its diameter is 6.4 billion miles.
- Close observers see a cold, slightly uneven, apparently flat solid wall. Known attacks cannot crack it.
- Temporary passages open and close unpredictably. Ships can cross them; phlogiston cannot. Conjuration can open a passage and closes the farthest natural one.
- A mage claims 3,200 passages are always open. This is an attributed claim, not a published catalog of locations.
- Enormous magical inscriptions cover the inner wall. Its apparent stars are openings to the quasi-elemental Plane of Radiance. These dangerous stars differ from passages into the phlogiston.
- Phlogiston entering through a hypothetical destructive breach would ignite atmospheres and the sun.

These facts come directly from [Dale Henson, *Realmspace*, SJR2, preview pages 3–4](https://d1vzi28wh99zvq.cloudfront.net/pdf_previews/17259-sample.pdf). The inspected excerpt does not specify a black material or transparent glass. Do not describe the atlas's chosen surface color as a verified material specification.

The original *Concordance of Arcane Space* calls phlogiston a fluorescent, multicolored, turbulent gaslike medium between spheres. Wildspace is the mostly empty interior; the shell separates these environments. [Jeff Grubb, *Concordance of Arcane Space*, preview PDF page 6](https://d1vzi28wh99zvq.cloudfront.net/pdf_previews/17263-sample.pdf)

The accessible classic previews support combustible phlogiston and its luminous, multicolored appearance. They do not include the full fire-damage rules. An exact explosion multiplier would require checking the complete rules, and is unnecessary for the atlas.

## Keep the edition choice explicit

The official 5e introduction describes travel outward from Wildspace into the silvery Astral Plane. That is a different visual treatment from this campaign's crystal boundary and rainbow phlogiston. The requested classic depiction should not silently become an Astral Sea scene. [D&D Beyond, *The Spelljammer's Guide to Wildspace*](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1238-the-spelljammers-guide-to-wildspace-in-dungeons)

## Proposed 3D treatment

The following are art and interaction choices, not additional canonical claims.

Use a dark blue-black spherical shell with a strong curved silhouette, a narrow iridescent rim, and restrained surface relief. Reveal the planets through a camera-facing cutaway or an explicit atlas transparency mode. A permanently clear glass bubble would make the boundary easy to overlook. The cutaway should visibly read as a diagram convention.

Keep a generous dark interval between the last planetary orbit and the wall. Preserve the published two-to-one radius relationship when using linear distances. If the existing atlas compresses distances, fit the boundary to that same mapping and state that the display uses diagram scale. Do not double an already compressed radius and label the resulting view physically proportional.

Build a few actual openings into the visible shell with masked or cut geometry. Give them a dark inner lip and a bright view into the exterior clouds so rotation reveals their depth. A glow drawn on an intact sphere will look like a decal. Use `Passage to the Phlogiston` for a selected opening. The user's word “breach” can describe the visual, but jagged shattered glass implies destructive damage that the cited description does not support.

Author a small set of visually useful opening positions and document them as illustrative. No stable passage chart or canonical passage coordinates were verified. Do not name these openings after destinations or promise that every opening reaches a specific sphere. The reported 3,200 count is no reason to put thousands of large holes in the overview.

Render the exterior as layered, slowly drifting clouds and curved currents in violet, cyan, green, amber, and red. Use local variation in density and brightness, with black gaps giving the clouds depth. Keep the phlogiston outside the wall; openings can expose its color without emitting gas into the planetary interior. Avoid ordinary fire, sparks, burning portal rims, and lava. Color and luminous motion can communicate its volatility without depicting continuous ignition.

Attach restrained points of starlight and sparse invented glyph motifs to the inner shell. They should move with the shell under camera rotation rather than behave as an infinitely distant background. Keep their appearance separate from navigable openings. Glyph designs and exact star positions are original illustration, not recovered magical writing or a canonical constellation map.

Frame the first view wide enough to show a complete rim arc, at least two passages, exterior color, and the planetary system. Preserve planet selection and orbit controls. Fade optional glyph detail before reducing the contrast of the planets or labels. A slow exterior drift is enough animation; a reduced-motion setting can hold it still.

## Review checks

- The visible title reads `Toril Crystal Sphere`.
- Rotation and zoom make the sphere's depth clear; its boundary remains visible against the clouds.
- Passages reveal the outside rather than merely glow on a closed wall.
- Phlogiston stays outside; the sun and planetary interior retain readable dark space.
- Stars on the shell are visually distinct from navigable passages.
- Help text identifies diagram scale and illustrative passage placement.
- The result still permits selecting Toril and the other bodies at typical Foundry window sizes.
